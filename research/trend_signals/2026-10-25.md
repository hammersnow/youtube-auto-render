## Proposed original family: `perfect_fit`
Hook: `WHICH PIECE FITS?` or `WILL IT FIT?`

Show one irregular socket and 2–3 abstract procedural candidate pieces. One candidate rotates/translates toward the socket while the viewer predicts whether it will clear the boundary. The reveal uses a deterministic collision/overlap mask: either a clean snap with a brief fill/glow and 100% fit meter, or a clearly highlighted collision gap. Variants can ask which orientation fits, which of A/B/C has the highest overlap, or stop the rotation at the exact fitting angle.

## Why it is distinct from current V7
Current strategy contains proven `wrong_motion`, `animated_maze`, `memory_shuffle`, `spot_difference` and experiments `physics_error`, `portal_path`, `laser_core`, `flow_field`. `perfect_fit` tests spatial rotation + geometric compatibility rather than anomaly detection, pathfinding, memory, trajectory, or field tracing. It also imports the satisfying snap/payoff pattern without reproducing another creator's assets or exact scenes.

## Free implementation
Python/OpenCV/NumPy/Pillow/FFmpeg only. Generate polygons / rounded polyomino-like masks procedurally; use affine rotation, binary masks, `bitwise_and`/XOR or IoU for exact fit validation, eased translation, glow/bloom, and procedural click/snap audio. No external footage, models, paid API, or proprietary game assets.

## Expected benefit
Combines an explicit prediction task with a satisfying visual payoff, giving a reason to watch through the final alignment and potentially replay to inspect the near-miss/fit. It is visually legible in the first frame and can produce large deterministic variation cheaply.
