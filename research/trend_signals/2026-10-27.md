## Proposed original family: `force_choice`
Hook in frame 1: **`WHICH FIELD STOPS IT?`** or **`A, B OR C — WHICH ONE SAVES THE BALL?`**

A glowing puck/ball enters a simple lane. Before motion resolves, 2–3 clearly different procedural interventions are visible: magnetic/attractor field strength, spring stiffness, fan/side-force zone, drag pad length, or bumper elasticity. The viewer predicts which option crosses a deterministic threshold: stop before a line, deflect into a target, or prevent a collision. Reveal overlays the force vectors / influence radius briefly, then replays the decisive moment in slow motion.

Do not reproduce another creator's geometry, magnet layout, artwork, wording, or result. Generate all parameters, shapes and outcomes procedurally.

## Why it is new vs V7
Current V7 proven families are `wrong_motion`, `animated_maze`, `memory_shuffle`, `spot_difference`; experiments are `physics_error`, `portal_path`, `laser_core`, `flow_field`.

`force_choice` asks **which visible intervention is sufficient to alter a known motion outcome**. That differs from `physics_error` (spot the physically wrong behavior), `portal_path`/`animated_maze` (route/path reasoning), `flow_field` (trace a field path), and earlier research `predict_path` (predict an endpoint with fixed dynamics). The controllable variable itself is the puzzle.

## Expected benefit
- First-frame comprehension: object + danger/target + A/B/C interventions are readable immediately.
- Physics payoff without heavy simulation: deterministic 2D forces are enough.
- Replay value: the force-vector/threshold reveal lets viewers verify why the near-miss failed.
- Large procedural state space: vary force type, strength, radius, velocity, mass, friction and target geometry.
- Strong visual upgrade path: field halos, vector streaks, deformation/spring easing and slow-motion impact can use the existing V7 glow/bloom language.

## Free implementation
Python + NumPy for fixed-step 2D integration and deterministic threshold assertions; OpenCV/Pillow for lanes, field halos, vectors and labels; FFmpeg for final H.264/AAC. No paid API, model, footage, asset, or GPU requirement.
